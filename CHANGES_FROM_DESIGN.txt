AquaScape - Changes from the One-Page Design Document
======================================================

Original design (one-pager)
---------------------------
A short, relaxing 2D side-scrolling platform hopper in a Y2K / Frutiger Aero
world. The chrome explorer ran automatically to the right (Jetpack Joyride
style) and the player pressed Space to jump / double-jump between floating
platforms, collecting bubbles, with checkpoints and a finish area. One level
moving through three looks: Blue Lagoon -> Glass City -> Moonpool.

What changed and why
--------------------
1. Genre: auto-running side-scroller  ->  top-down tiled exploration game.
   While prototyping I wanted the player to be able to slow down and actually
   look around the world, which an auto-runner doesn't allow. A top-down map
   made of tiles lets you explore at your own pace. It also gave me room for
   more interesting mechanics than "jump at the right time".

2. Jump / double jump  ->  Aqua Pulse + Dash.
   Jumping doesn't fit a top-down view, so the main verb became the "Aqua
   Pulse" (Space): an expanding ring that interacts with the world. It opens
   bubble clams, powers bridge pylons, lights beacons and lanterns, pushes
   glass cubes, and repels jellyfish. Shift is a short dash that also makes
   you briefly safe from jellyfish.

3. Checkpoints / falling  ->  no failure state, gentle hazards instead.
   Without falling there's nothing to respawn from. To keep the calm mood the
   only hazard is the Moonpool jellyfish: touching one bumps you back and
   knocks a few bubbles loose, and you can pick them back up.

4. Linear run  ->  an objective per area.
   Each area has a Prism Shard to find and a Beacon to light with it.
   Lighting a beacon builds a glass bridge to the next area:
     - Blue Lagoon: pulse a pylon to build a bridge to the shard's islet.
     - Glass City: a sliding-cube puzzle. Pulsed cubes slide until they hit
       something, and you have to land them on two pressure pads to open the
       glass gate in front of the shard. A cube pushed against a wall twice
       resets, so the puzzle can't soft-lock.
     - Moonpool: light three moon lanterns to raise the path to the shard
       while avoiding the drifting jellyfish. The last beacon awakens the
       Moonpool exit, which ends the level.

5. Music is adaptive instead of a single background loop.
   The soundtrack is made of synced stems (pad, keys, arpeggio, melody, plus a
   night pad). A new layer fades in each time a beacon is lit, and the pad
   crossfades to the night version in the Moonpool. Bubble pickups climb a
   pentatonic scale in the music's key when collected in a row. Footsteps
   change sound depending on the surface (grass, ceramic plaza, glass).

6. Same scene, three areas, as planned.
   The three areas are still one continuous level in one scene. Global
   lighting, water colours, ambient particles, ambience loops and music all
   blend gradually from area to area based on the camera position.

Unchanged
---------
- Y2K / Frutiger Aero chrome-and-glass art direction and the chrome explorer.
- Bubbles as the collectible, with a counter in the HUD.
- Title screen, pause menu and level-complete screen (with time, bubbles and
  a star rating).
- One short, hand-designed level (Tools/LevelGen/level.py ->
  Assets/AquaScape/Level/level.txt).
